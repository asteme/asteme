# What this folder is

The films and pictures that go with the project: `media/`. The README files at the top of the repository show the films (uploaded through GitHub's own editor, which is the only way a README plays one in the page); the files here are the originals, and the stills.

Everything in them is made from the conversation page's own preview with made-up scenes (`scripts/film/`). Nothing in them is anyone's real conversation or memory, and nothing in them is a thing the page cannot do: the words, the line to a star, the memory that opens beside the conversation, the promise under a reply, the day lines, the search and the stars are the page itself. What the film adds is a still at the start, a dimming for the days that pass, three lines of the README's own words, the hour of the promise, a pointer and a caret that keep the film's time, and the name at the end.

There is one film for each of three languages, and each is its own story, not a translation: `asteme-en.mp4` (a book put off until forty), `asteme-ja.mp4` (parents coming to visit, and a mother's knees), `asteme-zh.mp4` (leave for the New Year, and the couplets a father pasted alone). About a minute each (63 seconds the English one, 68 and 69 the others), 1920 by 1080. They share a shape, because the shape is what Asteme does: a worry at night; something said long ago comes back, and the person opens it and sees their own sentence and the day they said it; it offers to ask later; days pass; at that time it speaks first; and then everything ever said about the one thing that matters gathers out of the sky, a line at a time. `film-<lang>.jpg` is each film's first second: the still a page shows before the film is played.

The score is one original theme for piano, Asteme's mark read as notes, played a different way for each film and written to that film's own clock: it is heard when the memory comes back, breaks off as the days begin to pass, goes on from the note it stopped before when Asteme speaks first, and at the end each line that lights gets a note. The piano is the Salamander Grand Piano V3 samples by Alexander Holm, used under CC BY 3.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/).

To make them again: `scripts/film/README.md`.
